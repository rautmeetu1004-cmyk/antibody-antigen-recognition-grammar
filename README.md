# Antibody–antigen versus co-evolved protein–protein interfaces

A matched-pipeline comparison of **512 antibody–antigen** and **2,292 general
protein–protein** complexes, asking whether antibodies read protein surfaces using
the same chemistry as partners shaped by co-evolution.

**Headline result.** The two interface classes agree closely on chemistry described
by chemical class (r = 0.84) and differ at residue-pair resolution (permutation test
against a single-grammar null: observed r = 0.42 vs null 0.80, p = 0.001). The
difference survives matching on secondary structure, fold class, resolution,
redundancy, hydrogen-bond criterion and antibody format. It is localised to
**backbone readout**: both classes read side chains in identical proportion (0.72,
per-residue r = 0.98) and their side-chain grammars correlate at 0.49, but their
main-chain grammars correlate at only 0.14. The responsible chemistry is the
**tyrosine phenol hydroxyl hydrogen bonding to the antigen main chain**, together
with cation–π contacts, while aromatic stacking is depleted.

The specialised component of an antibody–antigen interface is the **antibody**.
Epitopes are chemically near-indistinguishable from the rest of their own antigen's
accessible surface (0.25, against 0.48 for a co-evolved interface and 0.97 for a
paratope), do not protrude, lie on a different face from the natural binding partner,
and avoid ligand-binding sites.

## Methodological notes worth reading before reuse

**Independence weighting is not optional.** The antibody corpus contains 45
complexes of one lysozyme cluster. Every statistic is weighted over 30% sequence
identity clusters of the bound protein; each contact carries weight 1/(structures in
its cluster). Unweighted, the analysis reports the chemistry of lysozyme.

**Effect size and evidence are separate channels.** Effect sizes come from
unit-weighted counts. Significance comes from raw counts, because a test needs a real
sample size and fractional weights manufacture significance.

**Size-matched nulls.** Restricting data to a matched stratum reduces sample size,
and small samples attenuate correlations independently of biology. Every stratified
comparison here is referenced to a null drawn at the same sample size. Three results
in this study would have been misread without it.

**Reliability ceilings.** Comparing correlations computed at different sample sizes
compares precision, not biology. Where subgroups of unequal size are compared, the
ceiling is established by splitting the larger subgroup at the smaller one's size.

**Verification.** 39 automated checks across `audit_phases.py` and `audit_full.py`
re-derive reported quantities by independent routes. These found five errors during
development, each of which produced superficially plausible output: duplicated
protein–protein contacts inflating Fisher sample size; a dihedral sign convention
that displaced every rotamer by 60 degrees while still passing a Ramachandran check;
asparagine N-glycosylation sites recorded as post-translational modifications; an
omitted sign in the heat-capacity relation; and a direction mismatch that compared
each protein against its partner rather than itself. All are corrected.

## Relationship to prior work

This study confirms and quantifies several established findings rather than
overturning them. In particular, that epitope composition does not deviate from
equally exposed antigen surface, and that epitopes cannot be related to a protrusion
index, were reported by Kringelum et al. (2013); that paratope aromatic residues
contact antigen main-chain atoms was reported by Peng et al. (2014). The contribution
here is the matched comparator that makes those null results interpretable, placing
them on a scale, plus the readout-channel decomposition and the distinction between
pose discrimination and sequence threading that reconciles our transfer result with
the antibody-docking literature (Brenke et al., 2012).

`FINDINGS_VS_LITERATURE.md` classifies every finding as confirming, refining,
contradicting or extending prior work. **Note:** that file predates the literature
verification and still overstates novelty in three places; the manuscript is the
corrected account.
